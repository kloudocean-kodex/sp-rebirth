# Sana Feedback / RISE V2 Research Brief

Status: research and redesign brief only. This file does not authorise production promotion.

## Why V1 was not good enough

The first RISE-inspired redesign over-corrected for simplicity. It reduced navigation and repetition, but the rendered homepage became too austere, too abstract and too generic-luxury. The repeated illustrated founder placeholder removed the human energy Sana explicitly responded to in RISE. Large empty editorial sections, tiny supporting copy, muted gold and repeated abstract accountability language produced a polished wireframe rather than a memorable founder-led brand experience.

This is a corrective wave, not a restart of the GrowthEngine architecture. SEO routes, lead infrastructure, analytics, accessibility, browser coverage, WordPress migration safeguards and production gates remain intact.

## Deep RISE findings

### What the client actually reacted to visually

The screenshots Sana shared show a dark charcoal header with a white RISE wordmark, compact uppercase navigation, orange active/accent states and an outlined CONNECT control. Page sections use light neutral backgrounds with large black headings and orange emphasis. Founder/event photography is not presented as ordinary rectangles: it is masked into circular or irregular organic cut-outs, creating movement and personality. Another visible RISE motif uses large dark cards for the letters R I S E with the active/accent card in orange.

The important visual grammar is therefore:

1. confident founder visibility;
2. real photography and real environments;
3. bold sans-serif hierarchy with selected words in the accent colour;
4. black/white/accent contrast rather than muted monochrome;
5. organic/circular cut-outs and layered graphic shapes;
6. visual modules that can be understood before reading paragraphs;
7. energetic cards, labels and large typographic moments;
8. strong conversion controls that remain visually obvious.

### What must NOT be copied

RISE is a mentor/training brand and its information architecture is large. Its current site exposes Home, Meet Your Mentor, multiple RISE Journey destinations, RISE Together destinations, a Growth Hub and Connect. Its homepage contains substantial long-form content, programmes, testimonials, media and multiple decision paths. Copying that structure would directly conflict with Sana's complaint about too many tabs and too much reading.

We should copy neither RISE's logo, wording, orange system, exact layouts, card artwork nor proprietary brand devices. We should translate the principles into an original Sana Patel Real Estate system.

## Sarah Cincotta's own property-management website audit

A public 2026 LinkedIn post from Sarah Cincotta is unusually relevant because she explicitly audits property-management websites. Her checklist says landlords need to be able to see who the business is, where it works, how it helps and how to make contact. She calls out missing About/Meet the Team content and photos, unclear service areas, unclear landlord information, missing contact forms and appraisal/discovery booking links, missing team videos and lead magnets, weak value propositions, absent reviews/case studies/FAQ, unclear next steps, lack of process explanation, generic/templated presentation, missing local insight and missing data.

This creates a better interpretation of Sana's request: remove cognitive overload, not substance. The homepage should make the first decision effortless while still providing credible depth through progressive disclosure and clearly grouped sections.

## Australian premium-property references

### WHITEFOX

Useful principle: bold, polished, matter-of-fact brand voice; high confidence; editorial photography; distinct image art direction; property and people treated as brand media rather than utility imagery. Do not copy its corporate structure.

### Kay & Burton

Useful principle: restraint, bespoke/personal service language, high-value property imagery and calm premium presentation. Do not inherit its traditional corporate density.

### Marshall White

Useful principle: property-management content becomes credible when it explains actual owner services, local coverage, process, proof and owner outcomes. Do not inherit its large-agency information architecture.

## V2 design thesis

The target is not "minimal luxury". The target is **confident, warm, founder-led clarity**.

The website should feel like a premium personal introduction to Sana, supported by serious property-management capability. A visitor should be able to understand the proposition from the first viewport, but the page should become richer and more useful as they scroll.

### Palette

- near-black / charcoal for navigation and high-impact bands;
- true warm white / ivory for reading areas;
- brighter champagne-gold accent for selected words, rules and controls;
- avoid muddy beige and avoid gold body text on white;
- use gold as energy, not as decoration everywhere.

### Typography

- primary display: bold modern grotesk/sans for decisive statements;
- supporting editorial serif only as a secondary voice, not the dominant brand voice;
- body copy must be visibly larger and more comfortable than V1;
- use accent-colour words inside headings sparingly, similar in principle to RISE's orange emphasis.

### Photography and placeholder art direction

Until approved Sana photography exists, placeholders must look like genuine editorial campaign photography, not illustrations or mannequins. They must be explicitly marked non-evidentiary in code/content metadata and never presented as Sana.

Required shot/composition language for the final real shoot:

- clean three-quarter founder portrait with direct eye contact;
- full/three-quarter standing founder image suitable for background removal/cut-out;
- founder walking or working in a recognisably Melbourne context;
- founder at a residential property/interior rather than generic office stock;
- portrait with negative space for headline overlap;
- seated conversational image for the story section;
- mobile-safe close crop;
- high-contrast wardrobe: black, ivory/white, restrained gold accessories if authentic;
- natural expression, confident rather than posed-corporate.

### Visual devices

- one organic cut-out founder hero, layered over a gold circle/shape and subtle Melbourne/property texture;
- one secondary founder image with a different mask (not the same asset repeated);
- property-detail photography as section separators or proof of context;
- large numeric/typographic modules for the process;
- review/proof section with clear source connection, not an empty widget placeholder as the visual centre;
- restrained micro-motion only below the fold and with reduced-motion support; hero remains static.

## Homepage information architecture V2

### 1. Header

Very small primary navigation: Property Management, About Sana, Reviews. Two actions: Call and Rental Appraisal. Supporting SEO/customer routes remain in footer/contextual links.

### 2. Hero

Answer four questions immediately:

- Who: Sana Patel Real Estate / founder-led property management.
- What: residential property management.
- Where: Melbourne and verified service area wording.
- Next step: Call Sana or Request Rental Appraisal.

Hero should use a real-looking editorial placeholder cut-out until the approved Sana shoot replaces it.

### 3. Immediate proof / orientation

A compact trust row using only verified facts: Licensed Estate Agent, founder-led, direct-access promise where already substantiated, Melbourne residential property management. Avoid tiny text.

### 4. "What do you need help with?"

Three visual choices only: manage my property; lease/appraise my property; change manager. Each choice should explain the outcome in one sentence and keep navigation scent clear.

### 5. "What Sana actually handles"

Concrete service clarity rather than philosophy: leasing and renter selection, rent/arrears, inspections, maintenance, renewals, compliance/process coordination, owner communication. Keep claims within verified scope.

### 6. Founder story / Why Sana

Strong cut-out or editorial portrait and concise founder positioning. Move the human relationship earlier than V1. Avoid repeating the same accountability message three times.

### 7. Melbourne/local context

A compact local-service section showing the verified service area and useful owner context. If suburb-specific claims/data are unavailable, do not invent them. This can later link to evidence-backed local search pages.

### 8. Process

Three steps with large numerals and a clear explanation of what happens after contact. This directly implements Sarah's own audit principle around an explicit next step/process.

### 9. Reviews / proof

Use live/source-driven review integration where available. Do not create testimonials. If the widget is unavailable, the fallback must still look intentional and link clearly to the source.

### 10. Direct contact + compact form

Phone is first class, but a concise enquiry/appraisal path must also exist. Mobile should keep tap-to-call prominent without becoming intrusive.

### 11. Useful depth

A small "Owner Guides" / resources module can surface 2-3 genuinely useful verified resources without turning the primary navigation into a content maze. This addresses Sana's desire for content people return to read.

## Content rules

- one idea per section;
- no paragraph longer than roughly 3-4 lines at normal desktop measure unless it is intentional long-form content;
- no repeated positioning sentence in multiple homepage sections;
- headings should communicate the point when scanned without body copy;
- use concrete property-management nouns and verbs more often than abstract brand language;
- direct phone and appraisal actions must be obvious but not repeated mechanically in every section;
- every destination link must have strong information scent;
- no fabricated statistics, awards, testimonials, local market data or claims.

## V2 acceptance tests

A first-time owner should be able to answer within seconds:

1. Who is Sana?
2. What property-management service is offered?
3. Where does she work?
4. Why might direct/founder-led service matter?
5. What does the service actually include?
6. How do I call her?
7. How do I request an appraisal?
8. What happens after I make contact?

Visual review must additionally confirm:

- no illustrated/mannequin founder art;
- no repeated hero asset lower on the page;
- body text is comfortably readable at desktop and mobile widths;
- page has energetic visual rhythm rather than large empty beige sections;
- gold functions as an accent with adequate contrast;
- organic image masks/cut-outs feel deliberate and original;
- property/local context is visible;
- desktop and mobile are equally art-directed;
- full Chromium, Firefox and WebKit QA remains intact.
